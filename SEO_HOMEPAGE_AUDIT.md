**URL:** https://getnelsy.com/  
**Date:** 2026-10-20

---

## 1. Search Intent Alignment

**Target query:** "booking app for nail techs"  
**SERP intent:** Commercial investigation — nail techs actively shopping for a tool to replace DM-based booking, collect deposits and get paid online.

| Dimension | Current state | Assessment |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| Primary intent match | H1 names "booking app" + "nail techs" | ✅ Strong |
| Deposit intent | Mentioned in ProductBenefits ("Collect deposits") and Transformation ("From No-Shows to Deposits") | ⚠️ Weak — copy is brief, no number/detail |
| Payment intent | "Let clients book and pay online" | ⚠️ Thin — does not name card/Stripe/online payment explicitly |
| Scheduling intent | Covered via "availability" + step 3 | ✅ Present |
| Instagram/social-first positioning | Mentioned in hero, step 1, Transformation ("From Followers to Clients") | ✅ Well-positioned |
| "Nail tech business" as identity | "BUILT FOR NAIL TECHS" eyebrow label — consistent | ✅ Good |

**Verdict:** Intent is broadly matched but the commercial depth around deposits and payments is surface-level. A nail tech evaluating tools wants to know: how much deposit, when is it charged, what payment methods are accepted. None of that is on the page.

## 2. Title / H1 / Meta Description

### Title
```
Nail Tech Booking App | Bookings, Deposits & Payments | Nelsy
```
- Primary keyword leads ✅
- "Bookings, Deposits & Payments" covers the three core commercial jobs ✅
- Brand at end ✅
- Length: 63 chars — within 60-65 sweet spot ✅
- **No issue.**

### H1
```
The Booking App Built for Nail Techs.
```
- Contains target keyword ("Booking App", "Nail Techs") ✅
- "Built for" is slightly passive vs. "The #1 Booking App for Nail Techs" but acceptable for brand voice ✅
- Does **not** mention deposits or payments — the secondary commercial jobs that are in the title but absent from H1 ⚠️

**Recommendation:** H1 is acceptable. If A/B testing, try:  
> "Booking, Deposits & Payments — Built for Nail Techs."  
This aligns H1 more closely with the title and covers more intent in the first sentence Google reads.

### Meta Description
```
Nelsy is a booking app built for nail techs. Create your branded booking page, accept deposits and payments, manage appointments and turn Instagram followers into clients.
```
- Covers: booking page, deposits, payments, appointments, Instagram ✅
- Contains CTA direction ("Create your branded booking page") ✅
- Length: 170 chars — slightly over Google's ~155 display limit; may be truncated on mobile ⚠️

**Recommendation:** Trim to ~150 chars:  
> "The booking app built for nail techs. Accept deposits, take payments, manage your schedule and turn Instagram followers into paying clients."

## 7. Key Messaging Assessment

### Does the page clearly communicate the five jobs?

| Job | Present | Quality |
|-----|---------|---------|
| Bookings | ✅ | Strong |
| Deposits | ✅ | Weak — no specifics on how deposit works, amount, timing |
| Payments | ✅ | Weak — "pay online" is vague; no mention of card/Stripe/instant payout |
| Scheduling | ✅ | Adequate |
| Instagram/social-first | ✅ | Strong — woven throughout |

### Is the copy too vague / SaaS-generic / "link in bio" focused?

**Too "link in bio" focused:** Yes, partially. Three references to "link in bio" and "one link" frame Nelsy as a Linktree-style tool. The actual value proposition is scheduling + deposits + payments, which is more powerful. "Link in bio" should be the *distribution mechanism*, not the product identity.

- "Everything your clients need. One link." → reads as a link-aggregator
- "FROM LINK IN BIO TO PAID APPOINTMENTS" → correct framing (link is the channel, bookings are the outcome) but the H2 doesn't reinforce it

**Too SaaS-generic in H2s:** Yes. "Three things that change everything", "Your brand. Your clients. Your business.", "One simple plan. Everything you need." — these could come from any SaaS product. None of them mention nail techs, deposits, scheduling, or any vertical-specific concept.

**Not SaaS-generic enough in features:** The ProductBenefits section lists "Services, Availability, Deposits, Payments" with one-line descriptions. A competing tool like GlossGenius or Booksy would describe *how* each works and why it matters for nail techs specifically.

## 8. Sections That Should Be Rewritten for SEO (Without Hurting Conversion)

### Priority 1: ProductBenefits H2 + item descriptions
**Current:** "Everything your clients need. One link."  
**Problem:** Zero keyword value. One-line descriptions are too thin.  
**Recommended H2:** "Nail Tech Tools That Run Your Business."  
**Expand each card** with a second sentence of 10–15 words:
- Deposits: "Set your deposit amount and collect it automatically before every appointment."
- Payments: "Accept card payments online — no chasing clients after their appointment."
- Availability: "Set your hours and block off time. Clients only see when you're available."

### Priority 2: ComparisonTable H2
**Current:** "Your brand. Your clients. Your business."  
**Problem:** No keywords, competitor comparison angle is wasted.  
**Recommended H2:** "Why Nail Techs Choose Nelsy Over DMs and Generic Booking Apps."  
**Expand:** Add Fresha/Booksy as comparison columns in a future update (that's a conversion and SEO win simultaneously).

### Priority 3: HowItWorks H3 step titles
**Current:** "Share your link", "They choose a service", "They pick a time", "They book and pay"  
**Problem:** No nail-tech-specific language, no keyword reinforcement.  
**Recommended:**
- "Add your Nelsy link to Instagram" (signals the Instagram use case to crawlers)
- "Clients browse your services and prices" (services + prices are searched terms)
- "They choose a date and time" (fine as-is)
- "They book, pay or leave a deposit" (names the deposit explicitly in a step)

### Priority 4: ZeroCommission section
**Current:** Visually strong but copy is thin: "Nelsy doesn't take a cut of your bookings."  
**Problem:** Missed conversion trigger. This is where cost-sensitive nail techs (comparing to Fresha's 0% model) need a clear statement.  
**Recommended addition:** Add a one-paragraph explanation below the 0% visual:  
> "Fresha charges studios for advanced features. Booksy takes a per-booking fee. With Nelsy, your subscription is flat-rate — you keep every dollar your clients pay you, minus standard card processing fees."  
This paragraph: (1) mentions competitors by name for SEO; (2) converts price-sensitive visitors; (3) adds 60 words of keyword-relevant copy.

### Priority 5: FinalCTA section
**Current H2:** "Your next client could already be following you."  
**Problem:** Clever but vague. Not keyword-relevant.  
**Recommended alternative:** Keep current H2 (it's good for conversion), but add a `<p>` before it that functions as a keyword-rich supporting statement:  
> "Join nail techs who've replaced DM bookings with Nelsy — a scheduling app that handles bookings, deposits and payments from one link."
